Project Overview

This capstone project acompases three quarters worth of researching and application of web design. While choosing a project I went through a brainstorming process. I knew I wanted to work around the theme of mental health, but I was unsure how to apply it. I stumbled upon the challenging mental model of being okay with boredom. I found this topic interesting to me because I never thought boredom was a fun thing until I realized where it can take you.

Boredom affects everyone when they find what they are doing has no meaning. This can be doing nothing, waiting in line, waiting at the stop sign, etc. Boredom leads us to find meaning in the meaningless. Unfortunetly we try to find that meaning in Instagram, TikTok, Youtube. Can we be intentional with boredom to find meaningful meaning?

Users are invited to stay still and simply think in their boredom. Don't look at your phone, don't talk to others, simply be and enter in your thoughts. A randomized timer that looks like a walking stop light is set and after 30 to 120 seconds they are invited to share where they found meaning in their thoughts with others.

Project Research

My first research task was to do a comparative analysis, which you can find here. I took some creative inspiration from The Useless Web. There is a simplicity to the design and I thought the idea of including 'please' as the text in the button is a fun way to ask for boredom.

There are many misconceptions about boredom. Danckert explores the truth about boredom. Boredom is not a state of apathy but a space for yearning. Danckert distinguishes boredom and depression. Boredom is dissatisfaction turned outward, and depression is dissatisfaction turned inward. Boredom breeds outward action, and depression breeds inward, meaningless comments. Boredom leads to exploration for something better. Overview of James Danckert's research.

Visual Design

The waiting screen has gone through lots of development. At first I had the waiting screen look more like a traffic pole with a walking sign attached to it. After some user testing I found that the visuals were too stimulating. The timer was big on the screen and there was too much constrast. To remedy these problems I reduced the visuals on the screen and lowered the constrast by having the background turn dark. Now when a user is on the screen it is less stimulating.

User Interface

Before, when a user pressed the 'please button' the screen would instantly transition to the main waiting screen. When users were on the waiting screen, one person mentioned they were anxious because they didn't know what was going to happen. So I decided to add in more context. Now, once a user presses the 'please' button they are brought over to the pop-up screen that provides more context to the experiment and even encourages them to enter into full screen to have less stimulating visuals in their view.

Revisions

When users enter into the bordeom/waiting room there was the instinct to click around to find something to interact with. They were clicking around the timer and on the human icon inside the timer. One user keep reloading the page after 15-30 seconds so they never waited long enough for the exit button to appear. These were the findings that influenced my user interface decisions such as the one mentioned above.

If I had more time I would experiment with changing the waiting room to make it more interactive yet still provided the needed boredom. The timer would count down and random blue gradient dots would appear on screen. If the user presses the dots the timer would increase by 30 seconds or so forcing the user to not click around and to stay put.

Summary

I learned how to design to invoke thinking. Often design today in a capitalistic world is here to simply please us and doesn't provoke challenge or thought. By desiging with the theme of a challenge mental model I can use my expertise to invoke actual reflection and thought and produce an experience that people will remember.

I'm most proud of the fact that I was early on able to find my idea of my capstone project and yet keep it refined and simple. This is my last design project I will be doing in school so by now I knew that it's important to know your limits and to schedule your time properly. There were times where I was falling behind because of my busy schedule, so knowing what I could and could not do was incredibly helpful.
